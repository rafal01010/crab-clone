# Experiment procedures and delivery budgets

These procedures define observations and decision rules. The handling, network, selection, and layout comparisons below have run. No capacity benchmark or human playtest has run. Every numerical limit below is a proposed acceptance target. None is a measured capacity claim.

## Hardware and network profiles

The user requested assumptions for a lower-end Intel integrated-graphics laptop. The target profile is an assumed Intel Core i5-8250U, Intel UHD 620, 8 GB RAM, SSD, Windows 11, and Chrome 157 at 1366 by 768. The host runs Node 24.22.3 and also renders its own player view. Three additional laptops use the same assumed profile. This profile is a delivery target, not an observed inventory of the user's devices. Exact OS build, browser patch version, power mode, and driver version must accompany each result.

The observed development machine on 2026-11-14 is a Mac mini with Apple M4, 10 CPU cores, 10 GPU cores, 32 GB RAM, macOS 15.4.1 build 24E263, Chrome 157.0.8103.78, Node 24.22.3, and npm 11.19.1. This machine cannot establish compliance on the lower-end target laptop.

The assumed ordinary LAN is one local router with no internet dependency, the host on gigabit Ethernet, and three clients on 5 GHz Wi-Fi. A second profile puts the host on the same Wi-Fi. Record actual topology, adapter and router models, link rates, packet delay, and loss before interpreting results. Do not substitute an internet connection for the LAN.

## Decisions and failed experiments

The handling decision used `node scripts/compare-hover.mjs` on the development Mac described above with a fixed 60 Hz Rapier step. The script compared a rotation-constrained dynamic cylinder with a spring-supported cylinder over the same scripted movement, wall, craft collision, knockback, resize, and airborne segments. Five runs per model varied the other craft's lateral offset. Raw samples are in [hover-comparison.json](hover-comparison.json). The chosen body uses a radius of 0.5, half-height of 0.25, target speed of 7 units per second, and acceleration of 24 units per second squared. Both candidates traveled about 6.04 units during one second of acceleration and stopped about 0.96 units later. In the recorded wall segment, their maximum center position was about 9.287 against the wall's near face at 9.75. The chosen body settled at approximately 0.25 units above the floor and remained level in these observations. The spring candidate settled at approximately 0.62 and dropped near the wall. Both moved while airborne and settled after the upward impulse. These observations favor the simpler constrained body. They do not establish player feel, frame-rate independence, behavior on varied terrain, or absence of all jitter and tunneling. Those remain checkpoint work.

The network decision used `node scripts/compare-network-live.mjs` on the development Mac. One Node Socket.IO client and one host exchanged messages over loopback with artificial delay, jitter, message drops, and a recovery outage. The experiment used the production movement and client interpolation and prediction calculations. It observed one scripted route per profile. [network-comparison.json](network-comparison.json) records the samples. The route includes a wall, and both presentation strategies converged after movement stopped. The prediction candidate responded earlier in this limited setup, so the client uses local prediction for the viewer and buffered snapshots for other craft. Prediction is capped at 250 ms without a snapshot and never decides gameplay outcomes. The experiment does not model TCP packet loss, browser rendering, four-client load, or actual LAN conditions. It does not establish the response budget or correction quality in play.

The selection decision used `node scripts/compare-protection.mjs` to compare one-, two-, and three-second exposed intervals after a ten-second window. [protection-comparison.json](protection-comparison.json) records the state transitions, displayed choices, and simulated action permissions. The prototype preserves the original deadline through a reroll, rejects its old revision, resolves only the displayed prompt at expiry, and leaves unseen prompts queued. The two-second interval remains the initial choice because it gives a definite exposed period without adding the longer delay of the three-second option. The exercise models permissions and queued rewards. Real damage, experience, allies, and combat systems do not yet exist in this path, so it does not prove their integration.

The choice composition compared horizontal columns with stacked rows in Chrome on the development machine. The columns kept each description beside its option and left room for the map. A separate mockup compared outlined HUD text with black-backed text over bright, dark, and patterned backgrounds. The black backing was more legible on the bright and patterned examples. Choice and HUD text use regular or semibold weight instead of the reference's ultra-light body weight; the HUD uses a dark backing.

The first visual comparison used short research summaries. A separate CoreText calculation compared wrapping for three longer paraphrased descriptions of Detachable Tail, Pollinator, and Poisonous Saliva at the layouts' estimated widths. [choice-layout-comparison.json](choice-layout-comparison.json) records the lines. The descriptions come from the [community evolution index](https://everythingiscrab.wiki.gg/wiki/Evolution) and the [Neoseeker trait reference](https://www.neoseeker.com/everything-is-crab/Evolution_Traits); their pinned-version fidelity is unresolved and they are not release copy. The estimate supports retaining columns, but it is not a browser capture. To reproduce it, download [Inter's variable TTF](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/ofl/inter/Inter%5Bopsz%2Cwght%5D.ttf) to `/private/tmp/InterVariable.ttf`, then run `CLANG_MODULE_CACHE_PATH=/private/tmp/ycce-clang-cache SWIFT_MODULE_CACHE_PATH=/private/tmp/ycce-swift-cache swift scripts/compare-choice-layout.swift` on macOS. This TTF and the bundled WOFF2 can differ by version. The comparison did not measure comprehension, target-resolution browser layout, reduced motion, or actual game-scene contrast.

A failed budget requires a diagnosis and one controlled change before repeating the affected experiment. Preserve raw failing observations. Record the limiting work and supported population, rather than describing the development Mac as the low-end baseline. Repeat broader scenarios only when a fix can affect them.

If source fidelity and a delivery budget conflict, propose the smallest explicit adaptation with its evidence and obtain review before changing product scope. Missing target hardware leaves hardware acceptance pending. Catastrophic host loss is an abort, not a timed match victory. No whole-match average can stand in for individual survival time or prove that early elimination feels acceptable.

## Source map scale

The reference game uses four biomes and generated map elements. The [developer 1.2 update](https://steamdb.info/patchnotes/24669494/) confirms generated world changes, and the [map reference](https://hinamizawa.ai/everything-is-crab/map/) describes the biome layout. Neither establishes map dimensions in world units or a reliable traversal time. Treat numeric map size as unresolved until measured against the pinned source version. Each player needs an independent view and enough space to leave other players off-screen.
